# Active architecture and interaction contract

The active scene is main.tscn. Its root main.gd owns the player and camera; its ChapterOne child owns chapter progression. New chapters can supply their own controller and world while reusing scripts/shared. The archive panels remain chapter-specific because their terminology and record semantics belong to Walter's case.

Shared modules: chapter_interface builds and scales UI; dialogue_sequence advances cards and invokes completion once; save_store performs full-precision JSON reads and atomic writes; accessibility_settings validates a versioned preference envelope and imports older flat settings; film_presentation applies the injected shader without changing case state. dialogue_lang/dialogue_runtime/dialogue_state are the flat-file dialogue grammar, its game-facing loader, and its persistent visit/topic/outcome store (see Dialogue authoring system below). object_lang/object_runtime/object_state are a deliberate sibling for static world hotspots rather than an extension of the dialogue system — a hotspot has no NPC-style schedule or topic menu, so its ids are expected to repeat instead of staying unique — sharing the same GATE grammar and evidence/outcome state (see Object authoring system below). portal_lang/portal_runtime/portal_state are a third sibling for travel points — a portal's defining content is where it leads, not what it says, so its step vocabulary adds a `GO` circuit breaker the other two formats don't have (see Portal authoring system below). prologue_presentation is a standalone CanvasLayer for the photographic title screen and prologue slides, deliberately independent of chapter_interface's dark modal. case_board/case_paper/case_threads render the colored corkboard presentation (warm paper, cork, brass pins, red twine) above the existing case-state/link/Perception logic, which they do not change. Chapter One currently assembles these modules and maps its settings controls to them.

CaseState schema 11 accepts versions 1 through 10, folding in a separate `DialogueState` object (visit counts, per-NPC topic completion, dialogue-sourced evidence/facts — see Live dialogue integration below), an `ObjectState` object (per-hotspot examine counts and completion, plus a case-wide `inventory` array for carryable items — see Object authoring system below), and a `PortalState` object (per-hotspot attempt counts and completion — see Portal authoring system below) rather than growing CaseState's own fields for any of the three. Received originals and dated supplements snapshot evidence, statements, and available source metadata. Old records without statement/source snapshots retain those omissions. Accessibility preferences remain separate from checkpoints. Save precision matters: checkpoint timers and camera floats must survive JSON round trips exactly.

The tunnel checkpoint is captured at entry. Death saves the dead state; retry restores the separate entry snapshot. Mid-encounter saves restore clock, exposure, position, flask relief, camera, and record. Reading pauses world time. No save operation should consume the player's live save during QA; automated checks select a separate slot.

## Provisional encounter

A deterministic 12-second cycle warns for 2 seconds, watches for 4, then turns away for 6. Continuous central exposure of 1.4 seconds while watching is fatal; leaving sight resets exposure. The outer route and right recess are physically safe regardless of Perception. Perception reveals additional route information. The flask narrows noncolliding shadow shapes even at zero screen distortion and never changes detection or movement.

The cough, primitive figure, wall shadows, exact timing, text, and geometry are blockout material. Playtesting must establish whether first-time players understand the warning before entering danger, recognize cover and retreat, and distinguish presentation relief from protection. Automated traversal cannot establish those judgments. The forced spill, glass rupture, combat, full audio, and fatal comprehension sequence remain future work.

## Dialogue trees from the short-story drafts

`docs/design/1) no_exit_wound_expanded.md` (canonical) and `docs/design/4) no_exit_wound_alternate_resistance.md` (alternate) are prose treatments of the same events the build already dramatizes — the same six victims, the same Odell exchange, the same Almy and gardener beats, the same tunnel encounter. What both drafts share, verbatim in places, is investigative material the build had not yet dramatized: the Ophion Club barman's testimony ("They stopped speaking of power, toward the end... She'll have us home... She's no different from any mother") and the boarded pantry door behind it, present near-identically in both versions. That shared material became the barman thread (`_barman_menu()` in chapter_one.gd, "barman"/"club_talk"/"club_devotion"/"pantry_lead" in story.gd) — a revisitable menu on the same model as `_witness_menu()`, unlocking one topic per prior topic answered.

Where the two drafts diverge is tone, not event: the canonical draft's Walter absorbs Odell's dismissal and lets his own file carry the disagreement silently; the alternate's Walter answers him to his face in the doorway. That divergence became `_odell_response()` — a genuine two-way player choice (not a state check) with a distinct recorded statement per option, surfaced the first time in this build where the player picks which of Walter's two documented reactions actually happened. Neither option is scored, gated, or referenced elsewhere; the choice is characterization, not a branching plot.

Father Behan (canonical-only material — the club's naming, the two declined invitations, "men inherit money and then invent a reason they deserved it") is a third revisitable menu, `_behan_menu()`, on the town street rather than the estate. `_town_observation()` now accepts a generalized `return_target: Variant` (supporting any `Callable` as well as backward-compatible booleans), allowing `_behan_menu()` to cleanly route its second topic through `_town_observation("behan_name", _behan_menu)` without duplicating card sequences or hardcoding witness returns.

The woman outside Kessler's shuttered shop (canonical-only) is deliberately not a fourth menu. In the source she leaves before Walter can ask a second question, so the build treats her as a single `_town_observation()`-driven scene like `gazette` or `exemption` rather than forcing a revisitable structure onto a witness who, by the story's own logic, doesn't stay. Her presence and departure are managed via the generalized conditional actor system (`register_actor`, `sync_actors`, `dismiss_actor`) inherited across `estate.gd` and `town.gd`. Her card and Behan's `behan_name` card cross-reference on the board once both are recorded — the same "two halves of an argument neither speaker knew the other was making" the source states outright.

## Movement latch and click-to-move (TDD v8 correction + one item TDD v8 missed)

TDD v6 hypothesized an input-latch fix for `main.gd`'s movement, found no supporting commit, and declined to credit one. TDD v8 corrects this: the fix is real, just uncommitted at the time — `main.gd` carries a `MOVE_LATCH_MIN = 0.15` constant and a `move_latch_timer` dict, set from `_unhandled_input`'s action-pressed events rather than polled, so a keydown/keyup pair that resolves within a single physics frame (synthetic/automated input) still registers as held for at least that long. `main.gd` also carries a second, related addition TDD v8's source read did not mention: click-to-move (`move_target`). A left click raycasts the world (layer 1) and steers the player toward the hit point, clamped into `movement_bounds`, until arrival or a keypress — an accessibility fallback for players who can't sustain a held key, and incidentally a working path for automated/scripted input. Neither gates on `Input.mouse_mode == MOUSE_MODE_CAPTURED`, since pointer lock is unreliable in some browser embeddings and gating on it would silently disable the fallback.

## Phase 2 Mechanics: Forced Spill, Combat Stagger, and Causal Spine

1. **Forced Spill Mechanic**: Entering the deep descent past the foundation triggers a rock-spur snag that tears Walter's flask loose into the dark below. Per Design Laws 3 & 4, this punishes hoarding without lying: the exact amount hoarded is recorded in `state.flask_spill_amount`, the flask is set to 0, and subsequent inspection of the flask in personal effects clearly details how many pours were lost and why.
2. **Tunnel Combat & Stagger Architecture**:
   - Walter carries a 6-round service revolver (`state.ammo`) and Kessler's boning knife.
   - Revolver shots expend ammunition and inflict non-lethal staggers center-mass.
   - Knife melee attacks scale stagger duration with Walter's Strength (`2.0 + strength * 1.5` for drowned, `1.5 + strength * 1.0` for cultist), honoring Design Law 11 (Strength modifies landed staggers, never accuracy or instant kills).
   - Enemy asymmetry: Drowned sailors are mortal and can be permanently put down with a brutal finishing blow once staggered (`state.drowned_dead = true`). Fully transformed cultists cannot be permanently killed ad infinitum; staggering them buys time to retreat or navigate around them.
3. **Diegetic Corkboard / Perception display**:
   - The case board diegetically reflects Walter's accumulated Perception (`2 + mini(3, evidence.size() / 3) + mini(3, links.size())`).
   - The board and notebook currently show `CAUSE UNRESOLVED (PERCEPTION N)` plus confirmed links and evidence-gated captions (for example the recorded count and, when discovered, the measured passage). They do **not** announce an undersea passage or summoned presence from Perception alone. A full evidence-supported causal-completion presentation remains future chapter work (see `docs/qa/SOCIAL_INQUIRY_PASS.md` and Design Bible Law 5).
   - Automated regression test `tests/phase_two_mechanics.gd` (`--qa-phase2`) verifies Phase 2 spill/combat/ammo mechanics; board honesty is covered with the dialogue/usability suites after the Social Inquiry pass.

## Object authoring system (September 14, 2026)

`scripts/shared/object_lang.gd` (grammar/GATE parser) and `scripts/shared/object_runtime.gd` (game-facing loader/renderer) form a deliberate sibling to the dialogue interpreter above, not an extension of it: a world hotspot has no NPC-style schedule or topic menu, so `OBJECT:` ids (unlike dialogue's `TOPIC:` ids) are expected to repeat, with the first currently-true `GATE` in file order winning — the same cascade dialogue reserves only for `TOPIC: default`. The GATE mini-language is the same grammar as dialogue's; only the exposed function/field vocabulary differs (`object_done`, `object_count`, `examine_count`, `taken`, plus estate staging fields `rose_bodies_removed`/`birch_bodies_removed`/`lounge_exited`). `scripts/shared/object_state.gd` persists per-hotspot examine counts and completion, separate from `case_state.gd`'s own `inventory` array (written by the format's `TAKE:` step). `OUTCOME`/`flag()` deliberately share `dialogue_state`'s store, so a decision or flag set from either system is readable by the other; `dialogue_runtime.gd::make_context()` gained matching `object_done`/`object_count`/`taken` entries for that symmetry.

`scripts/chapters/chapter_one_objects.gd` is the live adapter (mirrors `chapter_one_dialogue.gd`'s shape): it renders cards, presents real FORK choice buttons, commits `NOTEBOOK`/`EVIDENCE`/`TAKE` effects once a segment finishes rather than per-card (unlike dialogue, but matching every other pre-existing examine-card flow in this codebase), and refreshes a hotspot's hover `LABEL` and `points` visibility every time `chapter_one.gd::_travel()` rebuilds a location. A hotspot the player can click that resolves to no eligible state — a visibility/GATE desync, this system's analogue of an unresponsive NPC — fails loudly with an on-screen error naming the location and id, instead of doing nothing.

`objects/estate.object` (7 ids), `objects/town.object` (3 ids), and `objects/tunnel.object` (1 id) are live, migrated out of the previous hardcoded `Story.SCENES`/`Story.FACTS`-style dispatch in `chapter_one.gd`. `Story.FACTS`/`TownStory.FACTS`/`TunnelStory.FACTS` deliberately remain (their `SCENES` counterparts are the dead code): `g.facts`, built from all three, is still what the adapter checks before mirroring newly-discovered evidence into `case_state.evidence`. Tunnel's other, more bespoke ids (combat, flask state, travel branching) were deliberately left out of the flat grammar. See `docs/OBJECT_AUTHORING.md` for the full authoring reference and `docs/OBJECT_MIGRATION_HOWTO.md` for the migration recipe, current inventory, and known limitations (one-way hotspot visibility; unknown GATE fields/functions still parse instead of erroring). `tests/object_lang_flow.gd`/`object_template_flow.gd` are standalone grammar/cookbook proofs; `tests/object_content_flow.gd` audits the real shipped `.object` files and drives a live FORK through the adapter.

## Portal authoring system (September 14, 2026)

`scripts/shared/portal_lang.gd`/`portal_runtime.gd` are a third sibling to the dialogue/object interpreters above, not built on either: a travel point's defining content is *where it leads*, not what it says, so alongside the same shared GATE grammar and `PORTAL:` id-cascade convention objects already established, its step vocabulary adds one thing neither other format has — `GO: destination | x,y,z | yaw | flags`. Reaching a `GO` halts step processing immediately and hands control back to the caller, exactly like `FORK`, just with no choice to make; only `chapter_one_portals.gd` — never the shared runtime — actually calls `_travel()`. Content authored after a `GO` renders as arrival narration once the caller resumes via a new `Runtime.after_go()`, the `GO`-specific counterpart to `resume()`.

`scripts/shared/portal_state.gd` persists per-hotspot attempt counts and completion, separate from `case_state.gd`, mirroring `object_state.gd`'s shape exactly. `OUTCOME`/`flag()` share `dialogue_state`'s store, and `evidence()`/`filed()` reuse `dialogue_runtime.gd`'s helpers, the same cross-system sharing the object system established; `dialogue_runtime.gd` and `object_runtime.gd` both gained matching `portal_done`/`portal_count` entries in their `make_context()` for that same symmetry, and `object_runtime.gd` also gained `spoken_to`/`taken` so an object's `GATE:` can react to dialogue/portal facts, not just the reverse. A new `visited(id)` GATE function (reading `case_state.gd`'s `visited` array directly) was added specifically because it is subtly stricter than the existing `spoken_to(npc_id)` (which only requires a conversation to have begun, not fully completed) — `service_entrance`'s original gate needed the stricter sense exactly.

`TIME:` works deliberately in the portal system: an omitted `TIME:` defaults to 3 minutes (matching substantive dialogue topic defaults), charged once the first time this portal identity completes (checked via `portal_done()` before charging, mirroring `object_runtime.gd::commit_through()`'s own `first_completion` guard) — every crossing after that is free; an authored numeric `TIME:` override (such as `TIME: 0` for interior street doors) is respected. `GO`'s optional `flags` are validated at parse time against exactly `nosave`/`elapsed` (mirroring `_travel()`'s own `save`/`elapsed_travel` parameters); an unrecognized flag is a parse error, not a silent no-op.

`scripts/chapters/chapter_one_portals.gd` is the live adapter (mirrors `chapter_one_objects.gd`'s shape): an `_exists(location)` guard checks `FileAccess.file_exists()` before ever calling into the shared runtime, since `portals.interact()` is called generically for `state.world` on every interaction across dozens of locations, most of which have no `.portal` file at all — the normal case, not an authoring gap, so it must never trigger the fail-loud path or its `push_error()`. A small, explicitly named escape hatch (`_before_travel()`/`_extra_cards()`) handles the rare travel point whose post-arrival content or side effects are genuinely dynamic (computed from live state, not static prose) rather than forcing it into the flat grammar — currently `tunnel_exit`'s custody-result card and `state.tunnel_complete` flag, `lounge_exit`'s `state.lounge_exited` flag, `exit`'s `state.estate_complete`/`state.estate_visits_completed`/`state.rose_bodies_removed` flags, and `street_estate`'s Day 3 `state.birch_bodies_removed` flag.

Migrated portal definitions are live across five locations: `estate.portal` (`service_entrance` and `exit`), `lounge.portal` (`lounge_exit`), `lower.portal` (`route_speakeasy`), `town.portal` (`street_precinct`, `street_almy`, `street_room`, `street_estate`, and `route_post`), and `tunnel.portal` (`tunnel_exit`). `chapter_one.gd::_interact()` calls `portals.interact(self, state.world, id)` generically; `_travel()` calls `portals.sync_points()` per destination alongside `objects.sync_points()`.

An independent review (Codex) found and confirmed-fixed three integration defects in the first migration pass: `route_speakeasy`'s GATE could be bypassed entirely when reached from the contiguous town's shared exterior (where `state.world` stays `"town"`, never becoming `"lower"`) — fixed by checking `portals.interact(self, "lower", id)` explicitly in `chapter_one.gd::_town_interaction()` before the raw, ungated `estate.routes` dispatch; an immediate `GO` with no post-arrival cards never called `commit_through()` on its resumed segment, so it never actually completed — fixed by always routing the resumed result through `_play()`/`_advance()` rather than special-casing an empty card list; and an authored `TIME:` was applied after `_travel()`'s own transition telemetry rather than before, so the logged event reflected the pre-charge clock — fixed by reordering the charge ahead of the `_travel()` call. A second review round found two hardening items, both fixed: authored `TIME:` charged on every crossing rather than once per identity (see the `TIME:` paragraph above), and unknown `GO` flags parsed silently instead of erroring. See `docs/PORTAL_AUTHORING.md` for the full authoring reference, including the "known limitation" section on dynamic post-arrival content. `tests/portal_lang_flow.gd`/`portal_template_flow.gd` are standalone grammar/cookbook proofs; `tests/portal_content_flow.gd` drives every migrated route through the real `chapter_one.gd` adapter, including `route_speakeasy` from the real contiguous-town world (the specific scenario the first defect above required), town street doors, and a synthetic once-per-identity `TIME:` check.
