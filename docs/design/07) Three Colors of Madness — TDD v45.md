# **Part One — What This Document Is**

**This is the Technical Design Document (TDD) for Three Colors of Madness. It is the companion to the Design Bible, not a replacement for any part of it.**

**The division between the two documents is simple. The bible owns the what and the why — thesis, Design Laws, narrative, the meaning of every mechanic. This document owns the how — engine architecture, data schemas, production sequencing, and the specific open questions a playtest still needs to settle before a mechanism the bible describes gets its final shape. Where the two documents ever appear to disagree, the bible is correct and this document is wrong until revised — never the reverse.**

**This is v45, a full rewrite from v44 rather than an incremental patch. The document had drifted, entry by entry, toward the Historical Change Log's own style — dated "status update" paragraphs, "confirmed via git show" provenance notes, "see HCL (n)" cross-references accumulating inside prose that was supposed to state settled fact plainly. This revision restores the split v44 itself first proposed: this file holds only the current, settled state of the build; the separate Historical Change Log (outside this repository, in a sibling folder — see `AGENTS.md`, "Where things live") holds the revision-by-revision record of how the project got here, including the gate-tightening pass, the telemetry investigation, and every other dated correction folded into plain current-state language below. Nothing here is less verified than it was in v44 — every fact restated below either carries forward a v44 claim that was itself independently confirmed, or was independently re-verified against live source or a live test run as part of this rewrite (see the counts and suite results in Part Two, all re-derived live rather than copied from the prior revision).**

## **Scope of the Current Slice**

**The Ophion estate grounds (rose garden, service passage, tunnel entry), Pickman Street (boardinghouse, cobbler's shop/Walter's room, precinct, morgue), three neighborhood hubs (business district, upper-residential ridge, lower-residential quarter), a post office, and the waterfront: a playable outdoor hub with scheduled residents, its own travel time, full save restoration, and the offshore whaling station visible as unreachable scenery.**

The day/night clock drives scheduled-resident placement and, separately, the sun, directional lighting, fog color, and street-lamp visibility, purely as presentation — it does not govern body removal. Story progression does that: the rose-garden bodies clear after the player's first departure to town; the birch victims (the woman and boy) remain visible and unattended through at least a second estate visit and clear only once Day 3 has begun, with no accompanying scene or notification either time.**

Walter, narration, beats, objects, notebook entries, and system text are intentionally silent by design, not by omission.**

**Object-evidence content is authored in a parallel flat-file grammar. All eleven scoped hotspots (estate's seven, town's three, tunnel's one) are migrated and live, confirmed by a live re-run of `tests/object_content_flow.gd` this pass. Portal content is a third sibling grammar; ten portal ids across five files are migrated and live, confirmed by a live re-run of `tests/portal_content_flow.gd` this pass.**

**Not yet implemented: the fatal-comprehension ending, Chapters Two and Three as playable slices, gamepad support, full key rebinding, a full encumbrance/leveling system, and Ekon's later consumption of Walter's record. This is a build-status list, not a lore gap — the reference novellas (`08`, `09`, `10`) fully dramatize Ekon's use of Walter's record in prose; it doesn't exist in the build yet. # **Part Three — Architecture As Built**

## **Story Actors vs. Catalog Residents**

**`chapter_one_dialogue.gd`'s `allowed(g, actor)` function implements two genuinely different systems:**

* **Catalog-scanned additional residents (`extra_actors`, populated by `catalog.scan()` in `setup()`) are placed through `dialogue_catalog.gd`'s `slot()`. Ordinary catalog residents return an empty placement at night, closing for the phase; a small, explicitly named exception list, `NIGHT_ACTIVE` (currently `speakeasy_bartender`, both `night_owl_one`/`night_owl_two`, and `lamplighter`), stays available through the night phase instead. `SCHEDULE:` is a fully deterministic `(npc, phase) → location` lookup with no chance element.**
* **Story actors — Odell, the coroner's assistant (`assistant`), the groundskeeper (`crew`), the old woman, and the steward (`barman`) — have their own bespoke, state-driven availability rules in the same `allowed()` function, none of which reference the day/night clock: Odell and the assistant are available whenever `world == "estate"` and the estate isn't complete; the groundskeeper once the lounge has been exited; the old woman while in town and not yet recorded as evidence; the steward keyed to the lounge and whether Mrs. Almy has been spoken to. None of these actors hide at night.**

**The day/night clock re-schedules actors continuously (`populate()`), but a phase shift is deferred while a multi-topic conversation is open: `case_state.gd`'s `pending_dialogue_minutes`/`defer_dialogue_clock` accumulate a topic's `TIME:` cost instead of advancing the clock mid-conversation, and `chapter_one_dialogue.gd` tracks a `session_actor` that `allowed()` always permits regardless of what a mid-session phase shift would otherwise compute; the world only re-schedules once a conversation genuinely ends, via `end_session()` (called from `chapter_one.gd::_close()`). This prevents an interlocutor's slot from shifting out from under the player mid-dialogue.**

**Returning staff for later days are implemented: `odell_precinct` and `coroners_assistant_morgue` exist as live scheduled NPCs, recorded under their own distinct topic-completion identity so they never conflate with the original estate encounter.**

## **Three Separate Investigative Threads**

**Three separate threads, each with its own prerequisites, not chained together:**

1. **The historian/schoolteacher witness-count thread. `local_historian.dialogue`'s `crew_omission_followup` topic gates on `topic_done(local_historian, crew_omission_official) AND topic_count(crew_omission) >= 4 AND NOT topic_done(...)` — ask enough people, consistently tagged, before the historian goes further. `schoolteacher.dialogue` carries a related chain keyed off `evidence(naomi)` and `evidence(reader_omission_letter)`. This thread's own internal chain (historian → four witnesses → historian → schoolteacher) does not connect to either thread below.**
2. **The steward appointment thread. `chapter_one_staging.gd`'s `sleep()` blocks *sleeping* — not the first conversation — when `steward_visits == 0`, `intake_done` is false, or `evidence.has("naomi")` is false; while blocked, the player gets an "Get up" panel. Reaching the steward at all is gated separately, through `chapter_one_dialogue.gd`'s `allowed()`: the lounge itself only becomes reachable via the `service_entrance` travel action once `visited.has("almy")` is true, and the steward's dialogue is only `allowed` while `world == "lounge"` — so the real first-visit gate is "spoken to Almy, then travel to the lounge." Visit two is enacted, playable Day 2 content, culminating in the steward's second visit, ending on `THE STEWARD: "Come back tomorrow, and don't bring your badge."` Completing it sets `day = 3` and `steward_visits = 2`. The substantive third encounter is gated by `case_state.gd`'s `steward_ready()`, requiring all four of `visited.has("almy")`, `day == 3`, `steward_visits >= 2`, and `coat == "Plain wool coat"` — the plain coat specifically, not just the day, is a real and separate requirement. This thread does not depend on the historian/schoolteacher thread or the newspaper thread.**
3. **The newspaper-correction thread, gated on `filed()`, not `evidence()`. `gazette_editor.dialogue`'s `print_correction` topic requires `evidence(gazette_correction_terms) AND filed(eight) AND filed(naomi) AND filed(lodging)` and awards `EVIDENCE: gazette_correction_printed` on completion — this thread depends on paperwork actually having been filed and received, separate from simply having collected the underlying evidence. `mrs_almy.dialogue`'s `show_printed_correction` topic gates directly on `evidence(gazette_correction_printed)`; once available, showing her the slip has her read Naomi's name aloud and acknowledge Walter's effort, recorded via `NOTEBOOK: correction_seen`. `gazette_editor.dialogue` also now carries repeatable mid-investigation and post-correction default greetings for both coat states, plus a post-correction inquiry topic (`the_counter_slips`), closing a real gap where returning to the newspaper office after printing the correction and visiting Almy had no eligible topic and tripped the dialogue system's fail-loud EOF guard.**

## **Montage Removal / Day 2 Enactment**

**The montage sequence (`scripts/chapters/montage_still.gd`) is disconnected from the active flow of a new playthrough — its art and code are not deleted, just unwired. Day 1 is unchanged (intake, Naomi ID, first steward visit). Sleeping ends Day 1 and returns control on Day 2 at 6:00 a.m., which has real content culminating in the steward's second visit, relocated to the smoking lounge. Sleeping before that second visit is blocked with a redirect back to the lounge; after it, sleeping advances to Day 3 at 6:00 a.m., and the existing plain-coat third steward visit is unchanged.**

**Old-save resume is deliberately preserved: `chapter_one.gd::_load_game()` reads `elif state.montage_index >= 0: staging.draw_montage(self)`, gated entirely behind an old save already carrying a set `montage_index`, so the path is structurally unreachable from a fresh game while remaining intact for anyone mid-montage on an existing save.**

## **Instrumental Voice Cue System**

**The wordless instrumental voice pack is wired into every authored NPC line:**

* **`VOICE: cue_id` is authored immediately before an NPC's spoken line in the `.dialogue` source; the parser attaches that cue to the line as part of compilation.**
* **The runtime emits a `[speaker, text, voice]` triple per line. Chapter One plays the matching WAV when the dialogue card appears on screen, and stops it when the player advances past that line.**
* **A 144-entry manifest validates cue names during dialogue loading and automated content validation, catching typos or unregistered cue IDs. Instrument voice volume has its own independent mixer control, separate from music/SFX.**
* **Walter, narration, beats, object descriptions, notebook entries, and system text are intentionally silent — the voice layer characterizes NPCs the player interrogates, not the protagonist or the game's own narration.**

**The completed ElevenLabs Sound Effects delivery contains 228 source recordings; `tools/build_elevenlabs_voice_library.py` maps the broader trombone vocabulary and supplied violin mood takes onto all 144 stable game cue IDs, so every `.dialogue` file's `VOICE:` assignments remain valid without source-file changes. The same parser and runtime is the shared foundation planned for Chapters Two and Three; what each chapter still needs to supply before its dialogue integrates is a chapter-provided protagonist identity (replacing the hardcoded Walter label) and its own audio contract — see Part Four for the open question of what that contract is.**

## **`WEIGHT:` on `TOPIC: default`**

**Ships in `dialogue_lang.gd`/`dialogue_runtime.gd`, covered by `tests/dialogue_lang_flow.gd`:**

* **`WEIGHT: n` (a positive number) is authored as line-level metadata inside a `TOPIC: default` block. Authoring it on any topic other than `default` is a hard parse error; a non-positive value is a second, separate parse error.**
* **Multiple `TOPIC: default` blocks are allowed, and the first eligible block controls selection. If unweighted, it wins deterministically, preserving specific-before-fallback gate cascades. If it carries `WEIGHT:`, the runtime draws only from eligible defaults that explicitly carry `WEIGHT:`; later eligible unweighted defaults remain outside the pool as deterministic fallbacks.**
* **No-immediate-repeat rule: the runtime tracks the winning default's source line per NPC (session-scoped, not saved) and excludes it from the next draw's pool, falling back to allowing a repeat only when excluding it would leave the pool empty.**
* **Defaults never appear as menu entries, weighted or not. Non-`default` topic IDs must stay unique per NPC; sharing a topic ID across different NPCs still intentionally supports `topic_count()`.**

## **`FORK:`/`OUTCOME:` Resolution With Recap**

**Ships in `dialogue_lang.gd`/`dialogue_runtime.gd`/`dialogue_state.gd`, covered by `tests/dialogue_template_flow.gd`:**

* **Inside a `FORK:` block, any `CHOICE:` branch may additionally contain `OUTCOME: decision_id = value_id`. The chosen value is held as a pending outcome for the session and only committed to `dialogue_state.gd`'s `outcomes` dictionary once the branch finishes playing; `set_outcome()` refuses to overwrite an existing key, so a decision is immutable for the rest of the playthrough.**
* **`topic_available()` is pure `GATE:` evaluation — a topic with a resolved `OUTCOME:` fork is not excluded from the NPC's menu. `render()` computes which of a topic's `outcome_keys` already have a recorded value, and `_locked_fork_options()` filters the presented `CHOICE:` options down to only the one whose authored `OUTCOME:` matches what was already chosen — reopening a resolved fork's topic shows the full conversation again, with the fork menu narrowed to the branch actually taken. The rejected branch cannot be reached again.**
* **Replay is genuinely free: `dialogue_state.gd`'s `record_fact()`, `discover()`, `complete_topic()`, and `set_outcome()` are all idempotent, and `commit_through()`'s `TIME:` charge is gated on `first_completion`, so replaying the locked branch fires no duplicate effects and charges no additional day-clock time. No separate authoring is required to get recap behavior beyond `OUTCOME:` on each `CHOICE:`. Forks using only `CHOICE:` keep their plain repeatable behavior — this stays opt-in per fork.**
* **Captain Odell has one fork that stays fully one-shot (his automatic intake conversation): it lives inside `odell.dialogue`'s `TOPIC: default` block, so it retires via the ordinary default-topic mechanism rather than an explicit `OUTCOME:`.**

## **`CHOICE:`/`FORK:` Shared Interpreter and Consequential Outcomes**

**`FORK:` choices hardcode Walter as the player speaker and remain silent — no voice cue plays for a fork prompt itself; `VOICE:` immediately before `FORK:` is a parse error. The project extends this interpreter for Chapters Two and Three rather than replacing it — settled — so protagonist labeling and an audio contract must become chapter-provided concerns before their dialogue is integrated; what form that audio contract takes remains open (Part Four). `OUTCOME:` already supplies the persistent, once-only choice residue needed across all three chapters.**

Physical examinations use a separate blue-gray archival frame — a deliberate visual split between "talking to someone" and "examining something." The shared card renderer keeps the Continue control anchored to the bottom of the presentation frame regardless of text length.**

## **Dialogue gate-tightening (2026-09-22), one fix reverted (2026-09-23)**

**DeepSeek V4 Pro in Bionic's logic-gating review of the older, hand-migrated `.dialogue` NPCs found six topics whose `GATE:` clauses let a topic or clue fire before the evidence or story-state that should have unlocked it. Five were applied cleanly and remain live. The sixth — `mrs_almy.dialogue`'s `identify` (grants `EVIDENCE: naomi`) gated to `coat = plain` — was a real regression, caught by `tests/dialogue_content_flow.gd` and `tests/town_flow.gd` (via `--qa-town`) both failing at the same point once run individually rather than under the aggregate's timeout: `identify` is not like Almy's other substantive topics DeepSeek's reasoning was matched against — it's the topic that names Naomi in the first place, and the canonical Day 1 flow reaches Almy in the police coat straight from the precinct. `chapter_one_staging.gd` blocks sleep until `evidence.has("naomi")` is true, so gating `identify` behind the plain coat trapped a normal playthrough on Day 1 entirely. Reverted to `GATE: always`, confirmed by both tests passing clean afterward. The other five fixes are unaffected and remain applied: `county_clerk.dialogue`'s `ophion_settlement` now additionally requires `evidence(lay_lead)` alongside its prior `behan_name`/`ophion_myth_classical` branch; `post_office_clerk.dialogue`'s `ophion_club_mail` now requires `evidence(club_talk)` rather than firing unconditionally; `school_parent.dialogue`'s `the_reader_lesson` no longer double-grants `curriculum_abridgment` alongside `sanitized_textbook` (grants the latter only, since `curriculum_abridgment` is meant to be the historian's hard-earned reveal); `school_parent.dialogue`'s `the_covering_letter` now requires `evidence(curriculum_abridgment)` only, dropping the branch that let it fire off the cheaper textbook clue alone; `widow_kessler.dialogue`'s `club_standing` now requires `coat = plain` rather than firing unconditionally.**

**Downstream safety was independently checked, not just taken on the pass's own word: `curriculum_abridgment`'s sole remaining source, `local_historian.dialogue`'s `crew_omission_followup`, still exists and still grants it, so `the_covering_letter`'s new dependency is satisfiable. `postal_overseer_statements` and `club_foreign_freight` (now behind `ophion_club_mail`'s new requirement) have zero references anywhere outside `post_office_clerk.dialogue` itself, so gating them breaks nothing downstream.**

**A separate parser-bug lead from the same review — that `widow_kessler.dialogue`'s `coat_difference` gate (`coat = plain AND (evidence(kessler_carriages) OR topic_done(widow_kessler, club_standing)) AND NOT topic_done(widow_kessler, coat_difference)`) throws "Unknown function 'AND'" — was independently traced and found not to hold, confirmed by two separate traces of `dialogue_lang.gd`'s `_or_expr → _and_expr → _unary → _atom → _comparison` precedence chain: `AND`/`OR`/`NOT` are consumed as operators at their own precedence level before any identifier reaches the function-call dispatch, so `AND` immediately followed by `(` parses as an operator plus a correctly-recursed group, never as a function name. `coat_difference` was correctly left untouched. See `docs/qa/GATE_TIGHTENING_PASS.md` and the HCL for the full pass record.**

## **Revisitable topics (2026-09-23): no menu topic disappears forever**

**At the author's explicit request — wanting to revisit any topic later to check for a missed clue, the way he himself plays — a completed dialogue topic no longer vanishes from an NPC's menu. The runtime already supported this without any interpreter change: `dialogue_state.gd`'s effects (`discover()`, `record_fact()`, `set_outcome()`) are idempotent, `dialogue_runtime.gd`'s `commit_through()` computes `first_completion` before calling `complete_topic()` so `TIME:` is only ever charged once, and a committed `OUTCOME:` already locks a replayed `FORK:` to the one chosen branch via `render()`'s `locked_outcomes`/`_locked_fork_options()`. The only thing making a topic disappear was authors gating it behind its own completion, `GATE: ... AND NOT topic_done(this_npc, this_topic)` — a once-only-decision idiom `docs/DIALOGUE_AUTHORING.md` used to recommend and now explicitly tells authors not to use.**

**Two changes shipped. First, `dialogue_runtime.gd::menu()` now partitions entries: active/new topics stay on top, and topics already marked "· recorded" (via the existing `_menu_topic_recorded()`) sink to the bottom, so a long-lived NPC's menu doesn't bury fresh leads under old ones as the corpus grows — presentation only, no `GATE` semantics involved. Second, a corpus-wide audit (script-driven, not manual, to cover all 170 `NOT topic_done` occurrences across every `.dialogue` file without missing one) found and removed 55 self-referencing guards: 49 ordinary menu topics plus 6 day-gated story beats (the coroner's and precinct's day-2/day-3 tables), all of which now stay revisitable — the day-gated beats keep their `day >= N` gate, just not the self-guard. `NOT topic_done(npc, default)` greeting-cascade gates and cross-NPC gates like `topic_done(other_npc, other_topic)` were deliberately left untouched; they were never what made a topic disappear.**

**Author decisions taken before the edit, since these were judgment calls, not mechanical ones: menu ordering sorts recorded topics to the bottom (confirmed); day-gated beats are revisitable within their day window like any other topic (confirmed); the six shared `crew_omission` refusal topics (asking again just repeats the same refusal, but effects are idempotent so there's no harm in it) are revisitable for consistency with every other topic (confirmed); NPC default greetings stay a one-time cascade, not revisitable — "revisiting" a greeting doesn't mean anything the way revisiting an inquiry does (confirmed).**

**Every affected test assertion was found and fixed, not just the corpus: `tests/dialogue_content_flow.gd` asserted `almy_trust` and `father_behan`'s `club_invitation` must vanish after completion — updated to assert they stay in the menu with the "· recorded" marker, plus a new explicit replay-doesn't-duplicate-the-fact assertion; `tests/returning_staff_flow.gd` asserted the same for `odell_precinct`'s three day-gated beats — updated identically; `tests/dialogue_template_flow.gd` (which parses `dialogue/background_npc_template.dialogue` directly) asserted `example_account` must vanish — updated to assert it stays, recorded, plus a duplicate-fact guard. `background_npc_template.dialogue`'s own worked examples and `docs/DIALOGUE_AUTHORING.md`'s prose were both rewritten to teach the new convention rather than the old one. `tests/social_inquiry_flow.gd`, `tests/dialogue_live_flow.gd`, `tests/playthrough_log_flow.gd`, and `tests/dialogue_lang_flow.gd` were checked and found not to depend on any topic's disappearance (the first three test `topic_count()`/completion-tracking semantics, which are independent of `GATE` visibility; the fourth uses an isolated test fixture, not the live corpus). Confirmed via a clean full `python tests/run_all_qa.py` re-run (35/35) after the fixes, following an initial run that flagged `dialogue_template_flow` as a timeout — corroborated as a false timeout from concurrent leftover Godot processes, not a real failure, per this project's standing re-run discipline (see the gate-tightening regression above and the HCL).**

## **Object-Evidence Authoring System**

**Evidence like `lodging` no longer lives scattered across GDScript object-interaction scripts, outside the dialogue system and invisible to any dialogue-only audit. It is authored as a flat-file-per-object grammar in the same declarative style as `.dialogue` files: `GATE:`/`EVIDENCE:` are identical syntax to dialogue's, plus `TAKE: item_id` for pickup/collection, which dialogue files don't have.**

* **Parser/runtime/state: `scripts/shared/object_lang.gd`, `object_runtime.gd`, `object_state.gd`. `case_state.gd`'s schema carries `inventory`/`object_state` fields additively.**
* **All eleven scoped hotspot ids are migrated and wired: estate's seven (`wounds`, `eight`, `knife`, `watch`, `gas`, `register`, `shoes`), town's three (`gazette`, `lodging`, `exemption`), and tunnel's one (`tunnel_record`) — deliberately excluding NPC-stand-in ids (which stay in `.dialogue` files) and tunnel's bespoke game-logic ids (which belong in neither grammar). Real content lives in `objects/estate.object`, `objects/town.object`, `objects/tunnel.object`, dispatched through `scripts/chapters/chapter_one_objects.gd`, mirroring `chapter_one_dialogue.gd`'s shape.**
* **FORK/OUTCOME presents live choices through `chapter_one_objects.gd::_present_fork()`/`_choose()`. `LABEL:` drives hotspot hover text through `sync_points()`, which overwrites `points[id]["title"]` on every sync.**
* **`object_lang.gd::_evaluate_cmp()` fails loud (`push_error()`, returns false) on both an unknown gate function and an unknown gate field, matching `portal_lang.gd`'s equivalent behavior.**
* **Known, accepted limitation: hotspot visibility is one-way only — `sync_points()` erases a hotspot once its `GATE:` goes false, but nothing restores it if the `GATE:` later becomes true again. Not a live bug today, since every current `GATE:` in the shipped `.object` files is one-directional; would need addressing before any future object content relies on a hotspot reappearing.**
* **Documentation: `docs/OBJECT_AUTHORING.md`, `docs/OBJECT_MIGRATION_HOWTO.md`. Test coverage: `tests/object_template_flow.gd`, `tests/object_content_flow.gd`.**

## **Portal Authoring System**

**A third sibling to `.dialogue` and `.object`, not built on either. A travel point's defining content is *where it leads*, not what it says: alongside the same shared GATE grammar and id-cascade convention the object system established, its step vocabulary adds `GO: destination | x,y,z | yaw | flags`. Reaching a `GO` halts step processing immediately and hands control back to the caller, exactly like `FORK`, just with no choice to make — only `chapter_one_portals.gd`, never the shared runtime, actually calls `_travel()`. Content authored after a `GO` renders as arrival narration once the caller resumes via `Runtime.after_go()`.**

* **Parser/runtime/state: `scripts/shared/portal_lang.gd`, `portal_runtime.gd`, `portal_state.gd`, mirroring the object system's shape. `case_state.gd`'s schema carries `portal_state` additively.**
* **Ten unique portal ids are migrated and wired across five live `.portal` files: `service_entrance` and `exit` (`portals/estate.portal`); `street_precinct`, `street_almy`, `street_room`, `street_estate`, and `route_post` (`portals/town.portal`); `lounge_exit` (`portals/lounge.portal`); `route_speakeasy` (`portals/lower.portal`, phase/coat-gated); and `tunnel_exit` (`portals/tunnel.portal`, two destinations depending on `evidence(lower_foundation)`).**
* **`TIME:` semantics: an omitted `TIME:` always defaults to 3 minutes; every portal, whether `TIME:` is omitted or explicitly authored (including `TIME: 0`), charges its amount once, the first time this portal identity completes — exactly like `object_runtime.gd::commit_through()`'s own `first_completion` guard for the equivalent object case.**
* **Cross-system sharing: `OUTCOME`/`flag()` read/write through the shared `dialogue_state` instance; `evidence()`/`filed()` reuse `dialogue_runtime.gd`'s helpers; `dialogue_runtime.gd` and `object_runtime.gd` both have matching `portal_done`/`portal_count` entries in `make_context()`, and `object_runtime.gd` also has `spoken_to`/`taken`. A `visited(id)` GATE function (reading `case_state.gd`'s `visited` array directly) exists because it is subtly stricter than `spoken_to(npc_id)` — `service_entrance`'s gate needs that stricter sense.**
* **A small, explicitly named escape hatch (`_before_travel()`/`_extra_cards()` in `chapter_one_portals.gd`) handles the rare travel point whose post-arrival content is genuinely dynamic rather than static prose: currently `tunnel_exit`'s custody-result card and `lounge_exit`'s `state.lounge_exited` flag. This is a settled design decision, documented as a known limitation in `docs/PORTAL_AUTHORING.md`, not an open question.**
* **Test coverage: `tests/portal_lang_flow.gd`/`tests/portal_template_flow.gd` (parser/runtime) and `tests/portal_content_flow.gd` (drives every migrated route through the actual `chapter_one.gd` adapter in a live `main.tscn` instance, including `route_speakeasy` reached from the real contiguous-town world).**

## **Fail-Loud Guards**

**Design intent: stop broken or incomplete gate logic from letting an NPC or object go silently unresponsive, instead surfacing an obvious on-screen developer error. Implemented as one centralized check per system rather than duplicated boilerplate per file.**

* **Object-side: `chapter_one_objects.gd::interact()`/`_fail_loud()`. Fires when a hotspot the player can actually click resolves to no currently-eligible `GATE:` block. Lives in the chapter adapter, not the shared runtime, because an object file having nothing eligible is often *correct* (the hotspot may simply not exist yet) — only the adapter, which already knows the hotspot is clickable, can distinguish the two cases.**
* **Dialogue-side: `chapter_one_dialogue.gd::interact()`. Fires when `Runtime.enter()` returns an empty session *and* the NPC's menu has zero eligible entries *and* the actor isn't `"odell"` (the documented exception, matching Odell's one-shot intake conversation). The gazette_editor fix above (Part Three, "Three Separate Investigative Threads," item 3) closed a real instance of this guard firing in practice.**
* **Documented in `docs/OBJECT_AUTHORING.md`, `docs/DIALOGUE_AUTHORING.md`, and `README.md`.**

## **Character and Environment Models**

**All character, cast, corpse, prop, and exterior building models are Meshy-generated, produced under the author's own artistic direction (poses, silhouettes, materials, and which real-world references to build from are the author's calls) — see `AGENTS.md` for the full credit. Two corrections are consistently required after generation, independent of subject: per-model scale (Meshy normalizes every export to a roughly uniform bounding box regardless of intended real-world size, so every model below is tuned against a known reference — Walter's height, another NPC, a doorway — before integration) and texture size (raw exports ship uncompressed 4K dual textures; production assets are downsampled to 2K, or 1K where acceptable).**

**Named characters, each with a bespoke model and its own tuned `SCALE_FACTOR`: Walter (1.30x, ~2.21m, `walter_model.gd`), Captain Odell (1.30x, ~2.34m, `captain_odell_model.gd`, tuned above Walter deliberately so Odell reads as commanding and visibly taller), the Gatekeeper boy (1.05x, ~1.78m), the Club Steward (1.15x, ~1.95m, `steward_model.gd`, with a custom `Clean_Glass` idle animation that reacts to whether he's speaking or being spoken to). The Coroner and Coroner's Assistant share one model (1.18x, ~2.01m, `coroner_model.gd`) across both the `assistant`/`coroners_assistant` and `coroners_assistant_morgue` actor keys — no longer peer height with Odell, now that Odell's own scale sits above it.**

**Six generic archetype models (`cast_upper_man/woman`, `cast_lower_man/woman`, `cast_observer_man/woman`, 1.05x, ~1.99m, `cast_model.gd`) cover ambient and unnamed background NPCs, assigned per-NPC by a deterministic name/gender/location classifier (`cast_model.gd::archetype_for_npc()`) rather than randomly generated. A shared murder-victim model covers the six rose-garden corpses; a shared covered-body model covers Naomi Freeman and her son, at a distinct, smaller child scale for the latter.**

**Five quay/waterfront props (boat frame, cargo cluster, dock crane, dock shed, fishing boat) are integrated at `assets/models/waterfront/`, optimized from raw ~28–30MB Meshy exports to 2K JPEG textures (1.7–3.9MB each), and scaled individually against Walter at placement.**

**Exterior building/landscape model integration is complete across all five districts plus the estate, landed selectively rather than wholesale since the models' own folder/subject labels didn't reliably match their correct street. Every pass follows the same collision pattern: the original primitive shell is kept, hidden, and remains the actual collision authority (`Legacy*CollisionVisuals`); the rendered model is presentation-only, added under a `Rendered*Landmarks`/`Rendered*Frontage` node; routes, labels, schedules, and interiors are unchanged in every pass.**

* **Pickman Street**: three Pickman-labeled models placed for Precinct 4, Mrs. Almy's boardinghouse, and Walter's rooms.
* **Lower district**: a mislabeled "Pickman house" model placed as Dwelling No. 2, chosen for its landmark silhouette and its tie to the salt mender's schedule; Dwelling No. 5 deliberately left alone so its speakeasy cellar door stays legible.
* **Waterfront**: two of three available storefront models placed as the Harbor Supply chandlery and the fish-stores front, plus the five quay/hero props above; the third storefront model was deliberately left unplaced.
* **Business district**: the schoolhouse's procedural presentation replaced with a modeled equivalent.
* **Upper district**: Residence No. 4 given a modeled house plus a restrained landscape composition (one fence/gate, two hedge sections, two tree/bush clusters) — concentrated on one residence deliberately, so maintained grounds read as a class marker rather than repeating town-wide.
* **Estate**: landscape-only and deliberately conservative — the authored iron gate, rose-garden, and boundary hedges were kept as-is, since their collision/evidence/route behavior is functional; two generated tree/bush clusters were added at the outer entrance walls only, clear of every evidence space.

**See the individual `docs/qa/*_MODEL_PASS.md` files for the pass-by-pass record; each was independently confirmed against source, not taken on report.**

# **Part Four — Architecture Decisions Needed**

**Shared-interpreter extension for Chapters Two/Three is settled (Part Three). What's still open: whether the audio contract those chapters supply is full spoken-audio assets, an extension of Chapter One's own instrumental-cue system, or something else. Tunnel exhausted-resources fail state is still open and unspecified.**

**Conversation-time/travel-time scale. `day_clock.gd`'s `CONVERSATION_MINUTES = 30.0` only fires for a small enumerated list of legacy story-scene conversations, not ordinary dialogue-file topics; regular `.dialogue` topics go through `dialogue_runtime.gd`'s `commit_through()`. `DEFAULT_MINUTES` is `5.0` (a substantive topic with no authored `TIME:` costs 5 minutes), and every corpus-wide `TIME:` value carries a +2 adjustment from its original authoring, both landed in response to real playtest telemetry showing the estate-through-ending route running long against the original single-day opener's timing. `TRAVEL_MINUTES` (a flat district crossing) is `30.0`. This tuning pass feels better in spot-testing but has not yet been validated by a full end-to-end playthrough — that validation is still open. A further `WANDER_RATE` increase (currently 5 game-minutes per real-time minute of passive free-roam, a candidate rise to 7x) is under consideration but explicitly gated on whether it's still needed once the above is validated.**

**Bridge-crossing cooldown. `chapter_one.gd::on_bridge_crossed(bridge_id)` enforces a 60-second real-time cooldown per bridge id, so a player who crosses a district bridge and immediately reverses course cannot double-charge the clock by re-triggering the same 30-minute crossing within one real-time minute. No automated regression test targets this cooldown specifically — the aggregate suite passing confirms no regression, not that the window itself is exercised by name; a dedicated test remains open (Part Six).**

**Playthrough telemetry. A unique per-playthrough session id (no personal data in the log payload) tags a local event log: real minutes to the first meaningful objective, real/in-game minutes per district transition, distinct NPCs spoken to before each phase change, each completed conversation, real time from Day 3's start to reaching the ending sequence (`day3_bed_reached`, a legacy event name whose current meaning is "reached the final glass sequence," not "returned to bed"), and an optional two-question debrief. Events buffer and upload to a single Cloudflare Worker endpoint (`three-colors-worker.dejunai.workers.dev`), which dual-writes to D1 (queryable) and R2 (raw JSON batches), idempotent via a SHA-256 `event_key` in `INSERT OR IGNORE`. CORS is restricted to the real distribution origins (GitHub Pages, itch.io) plus `:5173` for local dev, deliberately narrow — a local Web build served from any other port has its telemetry silently and correctly dropped. `debrief` and `session_end` are built as one atomic final batch (plus up to 24 recently-buffered pending events, safe to retransmit) and delivered via `navigator.sendBeacon` with a `text/plain` body on Web, which survives page teardown in a way a normal request does not; a `pagehide` listener distinguishes a genuine exit from a BFCache suspension before firing a closing beacon. In-game return-to-title and quit actions now offer this debrief early, with a clear Skip action, without completing the story, so a player who exits before reaching the ending can still be asked. A `session_end` field, `dev_brisk_used`, is sticky true for the rest of a session the first time the developer pace toggle (`F3`) is used, even if toggled back off, and is threaded end to end into the Worker's schema. The pocket watch does not currently emit a distinct telemetry event. See `docs/LOG_PLAYER_ASK.md` for the full schema/privacy constraints.**
